Part One

Getting an account,
and getting in

There is exactly one way to sign in, and three ways an account comes to exist. Most questions in the first month are about this one page.

The sign-in screen

Opening the dashboard goes straight to sign-in. Two fields, and nothing else.

FieldWhat goes in it
Login ID or mobile number Either works. The Login ID looks like an email address but is not one — the Team screen labels it “a sign-in identifier, not an inbox”. The 10-digit mobile number on the account works just as well.
Password At least 8 characters. Set by whoever created the account, or chosen by the person if they requested their own.
Tell shop-floor staff to use their mobile number

It is the same number they already give customers, so there is nothing new to remember and nothing to mistype. Keep the Login ID for head office and managers, who are used to an email-shaped login.

“That Login ID or mobile number and password do not match”

One message covers both a wrong identifier and a wrong password — it does not say which, deliberately. Check the exact Login ID on Settings → Team before resetting anything: most of the time somebody is guessing at their login, not their password.

There is no “forgot password” link and no one-time code. A password can only be reset by somebody with access to Team or Stores, or changed by the person themselves once they are in.

The three ways an account is created

1. An admin adds the person — Settings → Team → Add staff

  1. Fill in name, 10-digit number, role and store.
  2. Set a password of at least 8 characters.
  3. Save. The account works immediately.

Give them their Login ID and password yourself. Nothing is sent to them automatically — there is no invitation email in the system.

2. The person asks for an account themselves

The sign-in screen has a sign-up side, so somebody on their first morning can start without waiting for an admin:

  1. They enter full name, a password and their mobile number, choose I am a… (Salesperson or Store Manager) and pick their Location / store.
  2. They see Request sent. They cannot sign in yet.
  3. The request appears on Settings → Team under Account requests.
  4. An approver presses Approve, correcting the role if the person chose the wrong one. The confirmation names the Login ID just issued — “Aarav Shah approved — Login ID …”. Write it down and pass it on.
Who may approve whom

An approver can only grant roles below their own, and only head office can approve a Store Manager. A store manager approving their own new salesperson is routine; a store manager cannot create another manager.

3. Pending assignment — an account with no branch

An account not linked to a store is listed on Team under Pending assignment, which says it plainly: these accounts are set up but not yet linked to a store, assign each to a store so they can start working. The section is hidden when nobody is in it, so if you cannot see it there is nothing waiting.

Resetting a password

There is a Reset password action in two places and either will do: on Settings → Team against the person, and on Settings → Stores against their branch. Use whichever screen you are already on.

Staff can also change their own without an admin: their own Settings has a Security card taking Current password, New password and Confirm new password, and a Contact details card for their mobile number and personal email.

Who sees what

The role sets the default. Head office can then narrow or widen any individual.

RoleScopeLands on
Head office Every branch, every screen, all reporting. The only role that can approve managers, and the only one that sees kaccha estimates on quotations.Dashboards
Area managerThe branches in their area.Attendance
Store manager Their branch — inbox, leads, team, attendance, stock — and the approvals routed to them.Attendance
Salesperson Their branch's customers and their own attendance. On Branch Requests they see only the requests they raised.Attendance

Everyone except head office lands on the attendance screen, which passes them through to their own home page once the day is started.

Narrowing one person — Settings → People & Access

Search by name or store, then grant or withdraw individual screens. Left alone, each role keeps its standard set, which suits most people.

There is also one setting for staff who should only ever clock in and out, described on the screen as “Only Check in / Check out, leave and regularisation”. Use it for cleaning, security or workshop staff who belong on the register but have no reason to see customers. The attendance screen becomes their home and they stay on it.

One withdrawal that quietly breaks lead routing

A store manager who cannot open Conversations is skipped when the system looks for an owner for a new lead. The lead reaches the branch and then sits unowned, with nobody notified. If leads stop being assigned, check this before anything else.

Part Two

Starting the day

Attendance is recorded on its own screen, not by signing in. What that screen does, what it asks when somebody is away from the branch, and how a manager puts a bad day right.

The attendance screen

At /check-in. Everyone except head office is taken here on sign-in, and it is the only place a day gets recorded.

What you see

A greeting and today's date, your last shift, and a Check in for today button. Once you are in it becomes Checked out and then Done for today; anybody only passing through gets Continue to app. Some branches also ask for an Attendance photo with the punch.

What it does about your location

What the phone reportsWhat happens
Inside the branch fence
“Within store range.”		 Checks in by itself as soon as the location is confirmed. Nothing to type.
Outside the fence Asks for a Reason, then records the punch and reports it.
A fix too vague to judge
“GPS accuracy cannot confirm your location”		 Offers the choice in as many words: continue with a reason, or wait for a more accurate GPS reading.
No fix at all
“We couldn't read your location”		 A reason is required. The punch is saved and marked “Location unavailable — recorded without GPS.”
The fence could not be loaded Also treated as unconfirmed, and asks for a reason.
Nobody is ever locked out

A punch is never refused for being in the wrong place. A phone inside a concrete showroom can be wrong by 50–150 m, and refusing the punch does not move the person — it only destroys the record that they came to work. The fence decides whether a punch is verified, not whether it is allowed. A reason is still required, because an unexplained punch from outside the branch is indistinguishable from one person punching for another.

“Skip for now” exists, and it records nothing

The screen has a Skip for now button, so somebody who needs the system urgently is not held hostage by a failing GPS. It does not mark them present — it only moves them past the screen. Anyone who skips every morning shows as absent all month with no reason recorded. If a month's attendance looks too thin, the register is the first place to look and this is usually why.

Who is told

Every punch the fence could not confirm notifies the branch manager and head office, carrying the distance, the branch's allowed radius and the reason typed. Head office is reached whichever branch it was, because the notification goes to everyone ranked at or above store manager. The person who punched is not notified — they know where they were.

Marking somebody present by hand

The Mark attendance dialog takes a Status, a Check-in time and a Shift / batch, defaulting to the store default shift. Use it for a phone that died, not as routine: every change is written to the audit log with your name on it.

Putting a flagged punch right

  1. Open Fix attendance. Flagged punches are listed with the distance and the reason the person typed.
  2. Accept it as it stands, or correct the record.
  3. Corrections needing a higher sign-off go to Approvals → Attendance fixes, described there as missed / wrong-punch corrections awaiting your decision.

Roster and pay

Roster & Pay turns the register into a payroll month. Driven by a Month written 2026-09, it shows Present, Off / holiday, Unpaid, Overtime and Net pay per employee, with each run tracked through Drafted, Skipped and Issued.

Part Three

Customers and enquiries

Six doors an enquiry can arrive through, one inbox it lands in, and the record that remembers everything a customer has ever done.

Where leads arrive from

The Leads screen says it plainly: leads from ads, the website, WhatsApp and the counter all appear here. Each door is set up once and then runs itself.

DoorHow it is set up
A Click-to-WhatsApp advert The customer taps it and WhatsApp opens. A routing rule sends the lead to the right branch — Part Seven.
Writing to the number Nothing to set up. Anyone messaging +91 72080 17690 is answered.
A form on your website Website enquiry forms: name the form, pick the branch, say what it is about, publish it and paste its link into your site. The customer sees a public page, then “Thank you”.
A QR code in the showroom Lead QR codes: choose the branch, optionally where it will stand (“Front counter”) and what it advertises (“Bridal collection”), and set stop working after a date. The customer scans, sees “Scan to enquire” and “Tell us what you are looking for”, and the lead arrives tagged to that code.
A walk-in at the counter Customer Check-ins → Log walk-in & visit.
Typed in by hand Leads & Follow-ups → Add lead.
The QR code is the cheapest one to start with

It needs no website change and no advertising spend — print it, stand it on the counter, and every scan is a lead attributed to that spot. Give each position its own code and you can see which part of the showroom actually produces enquiries.

Logging a walk-in

The dialog records Phone, Purpose, an Item code (scan or type the code), What happened, an Item note — the example given is “wanted it in rose gold, size smaller” — an Outcome, the Customer feedback / remark, and Remind me at, which creates the follow-up so the visit does not quietly go cold.

Conversations

The WhatsApp inbox, and the busiest screen in the system. Pick a thread on the left to read it, judge it and reply.

What happens before you open it

  1. The customer taps an advert or writes in.
  2. If they came from an advert, a routing rule stamps the thread with the branch that paid for it.
  3. The bot asks its four qualifying questions.
  4. The thread is handed to that branch's store manager, who is notified by name, and is marked as needing a person.

What you can do in a thread

ActionWhat it is for

Create Deal / QuotationTurns the conversation into a quote without retyping the customer. Takes a Deal Name and an Estimated Value.
Schedule Follow-up TaskA Task Title, Due Date and Priority — e.g. “WhatsApp follow-up for solitaire certificate”.
Add customer noteContext for whoever picks the thread up next.

Each message carries its own state — Queued, Sent, Delivered, Read — so “they never replied” can be told apart from “it never reached them”.

Why the message box sometimes will not accept typing

WhatsApp allows a free reply only within 24 hours of the customer's last message. After that the box is disabled and says so, and only an approved template can reach them. This is WhatsApp's rule, not a setting, and the clock restarts every time the customer writes again.

The bot goes quiet once a person takes over

After hand-over the thread is marked for a human and the bot stays silent in it. When testing, message from a number that has never written in before, or it will look as though nothing is working.

Leads, follow-ups and reply times

The leads list

Filter by Source, outcome, tags and a From/To date range, or search by name, ref or phone. When a filter returns nothing the screen says which knob to turn: adjust the outcome, source, tags, search or date range to see more.

Add lead captures name, 10-digit mobile, where did this lead come from, what are they asking about, address, birthday, anniversary and remarks. Birthday and anniversary are not decoration: they drive the occasion prompts that push a lead up the list.

A row is marked priority when there is recent activity, an imminent follow-up, or a nearby occasion — and the screen says which, rather than leaving you to guess why something is near the top. Open WhatsApp on a row starts a thread with that person.

Follow-ups

Follow-ups & Reminders is the day's list. Marking one done takes an optional remark — the example is “Spoke to customer — coming in Saturday to finalise.” A due date can be edited if they asked for later. Two standard follow-ups are tracked per lead, and the screen tells you when both SOP follow-ups are done.

Calling splits into My tasks and All tasks, and can also Send WhatsApp Follow-up. The phone icon hands the number to your own device's dialler — the system records that a call happened, it does not place it.

Reply-time promise

Response & SLA is off until you set it. Enter Reply within (minutes) and Tell a manager after (minutes), then press Start measuring. The table then shows, per conversation, when they messaged, how long they waited, who answered, and whether a manager was told. Until then it says no reply target is set and measures nothing.

Consent

Messaging consent is recorded per customer and per channel, split into Marketing and Service replies. Find somebody by name, phone or email and record what they told you; the history shows what happened, on which channel, recorded by whom and when. Campaigns check this for every recipient, so an opt-out is honoured without anybody having to remember it.

Campaigns

To message a customer outside the 24-hour window you need a template Meta has approved. New campaign then walks six questions in plain English: what is this campaign?, who should receive it?, who this reaches, what does it say?, when should it go out? and check it over. Audience rules combine as all of these or any of these. Afterwards each campaign reports its Delivery and per-recipient status with a Why column when somebody was skipped.

Products is the tab most shops forget

Writing down what somebody tried on and did not buy is the cheapest sales intelligence in the building. It is what makes the next call specific — “the rose-gold band you tried, in your size” instead of “any update?”

How a lead is scored

Every conversation is scored out of 100, so a manager can see who is worth calling first.

Only the customer's words are read; the bot's own questions are excluded, or “What is your budget?” would score every thread. Bands run from Early at 1, Interested at 30, and the top band at 60 — where the system asks for a person. A thread is not scored until it has at least two messages.

All of it is editable under Settings → Business Configuration → Lead scoring. Scores are stored when calculated, so changing the policy does not rewrite old ones — use the refresh arrow on the Intent panel.

Part Four

Quoting and selling

Pricing a piece, getting a discount signed off, taking the money, and handling what comes back.

Quotations

Two tabs: Quotes, and Custom Orders & Timeline for pieces being made. The list shows Quote #, Customer, Origin store, Status and Total.

Building one

  1. New. Enter the customer's name and number.
  2. Choose the item type, and the style number if there is one — the download arrow pulls that design's materials in automatically.
  3. Fill the materials table: gold, each stone, making.
  4. + Add item for a second piece. Each keeps its own metal, stones, making and discounts.
  5. Create quotation. GST is added and the total shown.

The materials table

Gold, each stone and the making charge are rows in one list, in the same order as the printed sheet. Every row has its own Dis %.

RowPriced asExample
Goldweight (g) × rate per gram, less its discount4.90 × 11,000 = 53,900
Diamondcarats × rate per carat, less its discount0.72 × 20,000 −5% = 13,680
Colour stonecarats × rate per carat, less its discount2.00 × 30,000 −5% = 57,000
Makinggold weight × making rate, less its discount4.90 × 1,200 −16.66% = 4,900

Those four lines total ₹1,29,480 before GST, matching the shop's own sheet exactly.

Quotations — stones, sizes and sending

Finding a stone

Type what you know — round, VVS, ruby, or the code itself. The box searches shape, quality and group, not only the code, and the carats box offers the standard per-stone weights.

Size, remark and metal colour

  • Size is set once for the piece — ring size 12, bangle 2.6, chain 16 — with cm, inch, or blank for Indian ring sizes.
  • Remark carries bespoke instructions: engraving, finish, a customer's own stone.
  • Metal colour is not asked, because the metal code says it: G14YG is 14K yellow, G14WG white.
If it will not save

The message names the row — “Item 1, stone 2 of 3: … needs its carats”. An empty row you added and never used is ignored. A row with figures but no stone code must be completed or cleared.

Sending it

Send Quotation on WhatsApp delivers it from the branch's own number. A quote can also be attached from inside a conversation, which is quicker when you are already talking to them.

Two things only head office sees

Kaccha estimates — rough quotes marked with @ — are hidden from everyone else, with a toggle to show them. The rate a quote is priced from comes from Settings → Gold Rate; a quote freezes the rate it was priced at, and if the stored rate is stale the system asks you to confirm today's before saving.

Discounts, approvals, payments and returns

Discounts

The screen states the rule: raise a discount on diamond or making; within store-manager caps it auto-approves, higher amounts escalate for approval. Selling, Cost and Margin sit side by side so the cost of the discount is visible as it is asked for, and a Required column names the approval level needed. Caps are listed per role as a Diamond cap, a Making cap and a Quote cap.

A rejection needs a Reason for rejection — “e.g. Margin too thin at this price.” The requester sees it, which is what stops the same request coming straight back unchanged.

Approvals

One queue, four kinds of thing, each labelled on the screen:

  • Discounts — diamond / making discount requests routed to you for sign-off.
  • Returns — returns, exchanges and buybacks awaiting approval.
  • Leave — staff leave requests pending your decision.
  • Attendance fixes — missed / wrong-punch corrections awaiting your decision.

Empty, it says nothing is awaiting your approval — so a blank screen is an answer, not a failure to load.

Branch Requests is the other side of this. Any member of staff can ask their manager, the area office or head office for a decision — a diamond rate, a price override, a transfer — and follow it by Ref, Status and who it is With.

Sales & Payments

Three tabs. Sales records the sale. Collections is the ledger of money actually received — Record payment takes an amount, a mode and a Reference (UPI txn id, cheque no.), and every row shows who recorded it. Reconciliation sets store reported against bank statement and shows the Variance.

Rows with nobody against them

Payments brought across from the old system are labelled imported from the legacy system — no Eclat user behind it. That is expected for historic rows. A new payment with nobody against it is not, and is worth asking about.

Returns & Exchange

Returns, exchanges and buybacks, with Photo intake so a piece's condition is recorded as it crosses the counter rather than argued about later. Anything above the permitted threshold raises an approval, and a rejection needs a reason — “e.g. Piece condition does not match the intake photos.”

Loyalty

Two tabs: the Gold savings scheme, with enrollment, a maturity calculator and members listed by Plan, Installment and Maturity; and Earn with Éclat, the points programme. Members appear whether they join at the counter or on your website, and movements can be announced to your own site over an https address.

Targets sets a monthly figure per store and tracks Achieved against it. Sales Performance is the Leaderboard and Incentives & Commission.

Part Five

Stock

What you hold, what it is worth, what is moving between branches, and what has stopped moving altogether.

Inventory & Stock

Four tabs: Stock, Rotation, Melting & Scrap and Reorder.

Entering a piece

Stock entry records a name and SKU (e.g. NK-22K-0142), metal and purity, karat, and the weights a jewellery business has to keep apart: Gross, Net and Pure, plus Diamond count, Diamond wt (ct) and Stone wt (ct). Then Tag price and MRP, and the compliance fields — HUID (BIS Hallmark Unique ID), Hallmark no. and Certificate no.

Fill in the HUID as the piece arrives

Hallmarking is a legal requirement, and the HUID is the one field nobody can reconstruct later from the piece in their hand. Entering it at intake costs seconds; finding it afterwards means going back to the supplier.

Stock can be brought in in bulk with Import stock from Excel, and many pieces changed at once with Bulk update status. A status change needs a reason, and the note field shows the kind of thing wanted: “Reserved for Priya Sharma, or flagged for melting”.

Reading the stock list

Filter by category, purity, status, age and store. The cards above give Aging distribution, Store-wise stock, Auto-reorder alerts and Slow-moving stock. If reorder alerts are blank the screen says reorder points are not set up yet rather than implying all is well.

Melting

Nothing appears under Melting & Scrap until a piece is sent there, and the screen says exactly how: adjust a piece's status to “Sent for melting” in the stock ledger and it will appear here.

Transfers between branches

Stock Transfers lists each movement by Ref, Route, Pieces, Status and who requested it, filtered as Incoming, Outgoing or In & out. Many pieces can be moved at once with Bulk transfer.

Dead stock

You define what dead means, per category: Dead after (days) and Warn after (days) — the suggested figure is 180 — then Save rule. The list then shows each piece with its Threshold, how far it is Over by, its Tag price and a Suggested action. Scan a piece tag looks one up from the counter by piece number.

Part Six

Reporting

The daily sheet filed from a phone, the dashboards built out of it, and the reports that send themselves.

The daily report, over WhatsApp

Filed by messaging +91 72089 12616. No app, no login, no dashboard.

Giving somebody access — Settings → WhatsApp Reporting

  1. Open the screen. Everyone in your branches is listed with their Access and when they last used it.
  2. Choose Set up on their row. A code appears and is copied to your clipboard. It lasts 10 minutes.
  3. Read the code to them. They send it from the handset they want to use, to the staff number.
  4. That handset is now theirs for reporting. Remove undoes it.
Why you never type their phone number

The number is learnt from the message carrying the code, so nobody can bind a handset they are not holding. The bot also cannot open a conversation with a number that has never written to it — WhatsApp only allows that with an approved template, and a one-time code is not something Meta approves.

Filing a report

  1. Message the number and say hi.
  2. Choose 1 — send today's daily report.
  3. Answer each question. Reply 0 for none, or skip where offered.
  4. Check the summary, then reply YES to file, CANCEL to discard, or DATE 20/08 to file it for another day.

What it asks, in the order of the sheet

SectionQuestions
FootfallWalk-ins · serious enquiries · conversions
Table A — counter sale Sale value, then cash · card · UPI · old gold taken and its value
Table B — customised sale Booking value, open bookings, bookings closed, amount received, then its own cash · card · UPI · bank transfer · old gold

It only asks what applies

Nothing sold at the counter? Those payment questions are skipped entirely. No old gold? It does not ask what the gold was worth. A quiet day is nine questions; a full trading day is twenty.

The two payment blocks are kept apart

Counter sale and customised sale each have their own cash, card and UPI, because the shop reconciles the two tables separately. A heading above each question says which table you are filling, and each block must add up to its own total or the bot asks again.

Amounts can be typed the way a jeweller says them — 450000, 4,50,000, 4.5L, ₹4.5 lakh, 1.2cr — and all mean the same thing.

Reading the numbers

Reporting & DSR

Two tabs — This view and All stores reports. Today's DSR opens the day's sheet, which can be downloaded as the standard DSR in PDF or Excel, or sent to a Recipient WhatsApp number. Reports filed by WhatsApp are marked as such, so they can be told apart from those typed on the dashboard.

Dashboards and Business Activity

Dashboards is the morning screen, with a Tasks card — New task takes a detail, a Priority (Low / Normal / High / Urgent), a due date and an assignee. Business Activity answers four questions directly: when it came in, what sold, what has not sold, and every document. When a window looks empty it offers the likely cause — try a wider period, this shop's history may be older than the window.

Management and Store Comparison

Management is the cross-business view: leads by source, by branch and by stage; conversations by channel and who replied first; follow-ups and calling; the floor; quotations by state; feedback; and conversion by source, by branch and by owner.

It refuses to show a half-answer

If a figure cannot be worked out, the screen says nothing is being shown rather than a partial total and asks for a narrower date range. A blank here means “we will not guess”, not “you sold nothing”.

Store Comparison ranks branches by revenue.

Reports that send themselves

Scheduled Reports: give it a Name, How often, the Hour at the branch, a branch, who to Send to (comma-separated addresses) and which Columns. You can also download or re-send the last completed period by hand. With none set up it suggests where most shops start: a month-end lead report is the usual first one.

Morning Digest tells each person what they owe a customer that day, at an Hour (0–23) you choose, using a named template. The What mine says right now card previews your own and says nothing owed today when there is nothing to send.

Feedback

Two tabs — All answers and Needs a person. A Google review link can be set so a happy customer is pointed at it automatically.

Part Seven

Administration

Branches, what your business calls things, the adverts, and the connections to Meta and your website.

Branches

Settings → Store Setup. Regions first, then branches.

  • Add region — e.g. West India, code WEST. A branch can sit without one, listed as no region yet.
  • Add store — name (Surat — Main), city, code (SRT-01), region and phone.
  • Edit store & geofence — Latitude, Longitude and Geofence Radius (metres). The suggested value is 150.
  • Add store manager — name, Login ID, mobile, and a password of at least 8 characters.
  • Reset password, and Close store when a branch stops trading.
Setting the geofence radius

Too tight and staff standing at their own counter are asked to explain themselves every morning: a phone indoors is routinely 50–150 m out, and the stored coordinate is usually a street centroid rather than the door. Too wide and it stops meaning anything. Start at the suggested 150 m, then widen a branch if its staff are being flagged while genuinely at work — the flagged punches on Fix attendance carry the distances that tell you by how much.

Branches arriving from a connected system land in an Import holding area as Branches to review, rather than going live unseen.

Opening a new showroom

New-Store Setup runs a launch as a project: a five-department launch checklist, milestones (e.g. T-30, Stock & dry-run complete) and Vendor assignments with amounts and due dates. Each project has a launch date and a project lead.

Advert routing

Match onUse when
Ad-set name contains The one to prefer. One rule per showroom, matching a word your marketing team already puts in the ad-set name — “Bandra”, “Udaipur”. Every future advert in that ad set routes with no further work.
Exact ad ID Pinning one advert somewhere different, or a campaign whose ad-set name carries no location.

Where two rules match, the more specific wins — “Bandra Broadway” beats “Bandra”, with no manual ordering.

Name matching needs the Meta Ads connection

A Click-to-WhatsApp tap reports the advert's ID and nothing else. Turning that into an ad-set name needs Meta Ads connected with a token that can read adverts. Without it, only exact-ad-ID rules can match.

A branch with no manager notifies nobody

A rule can send leads to a showroom that has no active store manager. The lead arrives correctly and then sits, because there is nobody to tell. Before adding a rule for a branch, make sure it has an active manager who can open Conversations.

If a lead arrives unrouted: open the thread, read the advert card, add a rule for that advert or its ad set, then Reassign the lead that already arrived. Existing threads are never re-routed automatically, by design.

Most of the traffic is Instagram, not Facebook

Placements are left to Meta to choose, and it has settled on Instagram: over the week before this manual was issued, Instagram carried 96% of the Bandra advert's views and 82% of Surat's, Reels largest of all. Worth knowing before judging an advert by how it looks in a Facebook feed.

Where advert money went

Ad performance puts Spend, Clicks, Revenue and Return side by side per campaign, with a Mapping column showing whether that campaign is tied to a branch. Lead capture failures lists enquiries Meta sent that could not be taken in, with why and how many attempts were made — so a broken connection shows up as a number rather than as silence.

Connections, messaging and records

Integrations

Grouped as Your connections, Available to connect and Not available yet, so it is clear what is on, what could be, and what is not offered at all. A connection needing work outside CaratSense says Platform setup required rather than failing quietly.

For WhatsApp you add a phone number ID from Meta and the WhatsApp Business Account ID from WhatsApp Manager, and each number records who writes to it. Meta assets can be registered by Type and Meta id with a label — what your team calls it — and Connection health re-checks each against Meta and shows when it was last confirmed.

Messaging Routes

Maps each branch to the number it answers on, and shows which number sends from where. With nothing connected it says no WhatsApp number is connected and points at Integrations first.

Message Templates

Each template with its language, what the provider says about it, and whether it can be sent. That last column is the one to read: Meta's approval decides it, not anything in CaratSense.

When a message does not go out

Outbound messages shows everything composed and its state. Waiting for a person is the one to watch, and it is written to reassure when empty: every outbound message is either delivered or already attached to a delivery job that will run on its own. Nothing is stuck means exactly that.

Channels

One table: each Channel, what it can do right now, why if it cannot, and when it was last confirmed with provider. The quickest answer to “can we message customers today?”.

Records, imports and the audit trail

Data & Imports

Tabs for Sources, Import a file, History, AI knowledge and Where data came from. Every import is listed with exactly what it did. Map a file's columns once and save the mapping — next month it is one click. Product Images matches a folder of photographs to products by reading the filename, with the prefix and suffix to ignore set by you.

Catalogue

Search by style no, SKU or name. Catalogue integration connects your website and the Gati connector, explains what each number counts, and maintains the visual search index behind image search.

Audit Log

Every sensitive action with When, Actor, Action, a summary and the branch, filterable by action, store and date. This is where a hand-marked attendance, a role change or a discount approval is answered for.

Ticketing

For when something in the shop is broken rather than something a customer wants: raise an internal ticket for an operational issue; it auto-routes to a resolver team by category. The example sets the tone — “POS terminal freezes during billing” — and the description asks for what happened, since when, and what you already tried.

Getting Started

Settings → Getting Started lists what is set up and what is still outstanding. Worth revisiting a month in, when the gaps are the things nobody got round to.
